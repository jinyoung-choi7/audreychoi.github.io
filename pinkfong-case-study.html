The Problem

Three groups, one shared gap

Traditional storytelling apps ask kids to watch. Nobody's asking them to think, choose, or wonder — and that gap shows up differently for kids, parents, and the company trying to reach them.

📖

Kids

Books and videos are passive. Kids absorb a story instead of shaping one — the opposite of how they actually learn.

Bandura's Social Learning Theory
👀

Parents

Minecraft and Roblox win attention with immersive play, but parents hesitate — screen time without learning is a hard sell.

Our differentiator: learning first
📊

Companies

Educational storytelling for young audiences is a crowded, fast-moving market — staying ahead means offering something genuinely interactive.

Market gap
The Solution

Empowering active learning through interactive storytelling

Case Study

What Mystic Messenger gets right

A wildly popular interactive story app where users chat with fictional characters in real time — reimagined here as a safe, age-appropriate learning experience.

Why it works
  • 1

    Interactive storylines

    Users chat directly with fictional characters instead of watching from a distance.

  • 2

    Real-time communication

    Characters respond as though they exist in real life, deepening immersion.

  • 3

    Multi-ending structure

    Different choices lead to different outcomes, encouraging kids to replay and explore.

Storyboard for Pinkfong

Three principles, adapted from Mystic Messenger, shape the Pinkfong prototype:

Immersion with AI

Real-time interactions let children engage directly with story characters.

Educational focus

Meaningful learning content is embedded in every character dialogue.

Customizable paths

Kids explore storylines tailored to their own interests and pace.
