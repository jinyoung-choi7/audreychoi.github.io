About Peace Pods

Peace Pods are private spaces inside StartX where founders and their team members can step away from the work environment to rest, recharge, and reset. We designed them from needfinding interviews, through prototype trade shows, to a tested and built solution.

I designed them with Sophie Cline and Sophia Awuzie for Design 121 as team Rock, Paper, Scissors, working with founders at StartX in Stanford Research Park.

Synthesis

Coding the interviews surfaced four tensions. Underneath them was a recurring conflict between a culture that promotes constant, visible hustle and a quieter, universal need to step away from it.

A culture of constant work

“The accelerator culture [in SF] promotes this idea of sleeping on a mattress on the floor, working 20-hour days. That's just not exciting to people.”
“You don't need to post pictures of yourself on like Twitter, like crushing like a monster at like 2am while you're making all of your employees sleep on mattresses in one room. That's just not the reality of startup culture.”

Promotion of discomfort

“When you create space too nice and too comfortable for people in a very difficult challenge, it's sometimes easy to get comfortable — and you can't be comfortable when you're in a zone where you make no money.”

Deprioritization of self-care

“I find that a lot of self-care, um, is almost like aftercare for when I'm having a hard time.”

The need for alone time

“I wasn't super keen on [startup culture] because... I've seen just like the intensity of that kind of lifestyle.”
“My perfect workday starts with coffee in bed and pure creative time — just for me.”
“I really, really love journaling. I feel like that is maybe my primary way to process and kind of de-stress.”

Meet Maya

Needs to unplug and recharge throughout the workday

The Problem

StartX is full of workspaces, desks, and meeting rooms, but there is no space that invites founders or their team members to rest or take personal time for themselves.

“[Startup culture] teaches... that you need to work a certain amount of hours... or your company is gonna fail.”
“We used to have a makeshift [nap room]... but now it's a meeting room.”
67%of 18 to 34 year-olds said stress makes it hard for them to focus
58%say that most days their stress is “completely overwhelming,” the most of any age group surveyed (APA, 2023)

How Might We

We framed the design space with eight questions.

How might we turn our workspaces into creative studios, not just places to sit at a desk?
How might we foster positive group dynamics in a high-pressure environment?
How might we allow people to share the new tools and resources they've discovered?
How might we protect quiet, creative time from the chaos of to-do lists?
How might we encourage flow of new ideas and insights?
How might we help people do their best thinking without pressure?
How might we allow mentor and mentee to exchange insights as equals?
How might we promote a culture of work-life balance in an industry that pushes the opposite?

Trade Show

From 27 concepts, we converged on three refined prototypes and tested them at a prototype trade show with founders and StartX staff.

The Here and Now Kiosk

A live, interactive display for spontaneous connection across Stanford Research Park. Tap any building to see who is inside and which events are open to drop-ins.

Feedback was that it felt exciting and novel, but people had privacy concerns around shared location data.

Startup Showcase Wall

A large, live, interactive display surfacing founder, product, and mentor updates, so staying current does not cost anyone independent research time.

Feedback was that people loved the convenience, but questioned multi-user access and long-term novelty.

Wellness Pods

Private pods lowering the barrier to relaxation during the work day, for a nap, a journal, a stretch, or a moment of quiet before returning to work.

Feedback was that it was the most requested by founders and staff alike, though some doubted founders would “allow themselves” to use it.

Doubling Down on Wellness Pods

The Wellness Pod prototype pulled the most positive feedback and the most engagement time at the trade show, so we refined our point of view and tested again with aspiring Gen Z founders.

Struggles to set boundaries between work and rest

Learnings from testing

Recharge, not retreat. Given StartX's focus on collaboration, this is not a solo work space to sequester in. It is for founders who need to recharge for short periods when their social battery is drained.
Comfortable, not too comfortable. The room should draw people in and be a positive addition to the space, but people need a reason to leave, with no devices inside, so the office stays social and collaborative.
Privacy over efficiency. Testers by and large valued the privacy of individual pods over the efficiency of multiple people sharing one space with flexible dividers.
Unexpected use cases. Testers surfaced uses we had not considered, like breastfeeding mothers needing to pump, or anyone needing privacy for prayer throughout the day.

The Final Solution

Peace Pods are a private space that allows founders and their team members to relax, recharge, and step away from the traditional work environment. They are the perfect place to nap, journal, doodle, meditate, stretch, or daydream.

Each pod comes with

Alexa, warm lighting, art, and plants
Doodle supplies
Journals and self-care prompts
A wide selection of books
Stretch, breath, and rolling exercises, plus a foam roller
Quick snacks for a pick-me-up
